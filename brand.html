Brand usage

Guidelines

How Catilda looks, sounds, and shows up. Use this page as the source of truth for the wordmark, the color system, typography, and the way we talk about her. Catilda is the employee. She gets a name and a pronoun: she, never it.

01 · Logo

Wordmark

The primary logo is the cat sitting to the left of the lowercase Space Grotesk wordmark (see 02 · The cat). The wordmark alone is for quiet, typographic contexts. Give it clear space equal to the height of the “c” on all sides, keep contrast high, and never set it in a colour outside the palette.

02 · The cat

The Catilda cat

The mark, drawn from the original sketch: round head, one ear straight and one cocked, pill eyes, triangle nose, whiskers that run past the face. Marmalade fur (#E8913F) with ink lines is the primary colourway. The line-only ink cat is for quiet contexts; mint lines live on dark panels. The cat sits to the left of the wordmark at roughly 1.5× its cap height.

Favicon & small sizes

Favicons and app icons use the full cat mark, marmalade fur with ink lines, on a cloud tile. The owner rejected line-only marks on a dark tile, so the same full mark is chosen at every size: it reads the same in a browser tab, on a home screen, and on the wordmark lockup.

Do

  • Favicon, OS app icon, messaging avatars: the full cat mark on a cloud tile.
  • Marmalade fur with ink lines everywhere the mark appears as an icon.
  • Keep the whiskers crossing past the face on the full mark.

Don’t

  • Don’t redraw, outline, add a mouth, or straighten the cocked ear.
  • Don’t use fur colours outside marmalade, or lines outside the palette.
  • Don’t swap in a line-only mark on a dark tile; the owner rejected that version.

Gradients

Six blends. Light gradients carry ink text and dark gradients carry cloud text. The tri-blend is for hero halos and celebration moments, behind content rather than under copy.

Contrast rules, all measured. Cobalt buttons carry white text at 5.7:1. Amber and mint are light and carry ink text only, at 9.3:1 and 10.6:1; white on either is under 2:1 and unreadable. Bright cobalt is for gradients and glow, never text or buttons. Body is 6.3:1 on cloud and muted is 5.6:1, so both clear AA. Washes at 8 to 32% are backgrounds for ink text and nothing else.
04 · Typography

Type system

Three voices: a warm display face for headlines, a neutral workhorse for reading, and a mono for the machine-flavored details.

AaBbCc

Space Grotesk

Display · Headlines · Wordmark

Weights 500 to 700. Tracking −0.02em. Sentence case, never all-caps. Space Grotesk carries the headlines, the wordmark and the numbers.

AaBbCc

Inter Regular / Medium

Body · UI · Forms

Weights 400–600. Line-height ≈1.6 for paragraphs. Inter carries all reading text and interface labels.

0123

Space Grotesk

Numbers · Stats · Figures

Weights 500–700. Every stat, metric, and dollar figure is set in Space Grotesk. JetBrains Mono appears only inside product-console mockups.

  • Headlines: Space Grotesk 600 to 700, tight leading (1.02 to 1.1), Ink.
  • Numbers and stats: Space Grotesk. Mono is reserved for the diegetic product console.
  • Never mix a fourth typeface into product or marketing surfaces.
05 · Voice

Catilda, herself

Catilda is the employee, not the software. She has a name, so she gets a pronoun: she, never it, and never the bot. Write about her the way you would introduce a capable new colleague to a client: calm, concrete, and useful. Plain sentences, real examples, no hype.

How Catilda sounds

  • Smart. She brings clarity to complexity.
  • Reliable. You can count on her to get things done.
  • Proactive. She anticipates needs and drives results.
  • Humane. She reads and writes like a person, not like software.

In practice

  • Say what happened and what it means, then stop.
  • Name the real job, never the category. "Chased the invoice", not "automated a workflow".
  • Offer the next step before being asked.
  • Write the way a good colleague speaks, not the way software writes.

Who Catilda is

  • A digital employee for small and mid-size businesses. Hired, not installed.
  • She works inside the tools you already use: inbox, calendar, documents, CRM.
  • She reads, drafts, follows up, reconciles, reminds. The busywork, finished.
  • Human-in-the-loop: she prepares, you approve. Nothing irreversible without your sign-off.
  • Priced and pitched like a hire: one employee, a fraction of a salary.

What Catilda is not

  • Not a chatbot and not “an assistant that answers questions”. She does the task.
  • Not another dashboard to learn. There is no new interface to live in.
  • Not unsupervised autopilot. You are always in the approval seat.
  • Not a replacement for your team. She clears the plate for them.
  • Not “AI-powered software”. Catilda is the employee, not the feature.
06 · Rules

DOs & DON’Ts

Do

  • Catilda is she, never it. One employee, one name, one pronoun.
  • Always say Catilda, never “the Catilda bot”.
  • Lead with outcomes: hours saved, follow-ups sent, invoices chased.
  • Use hiring language: “hire”, “onboard”, “delegate”, “your new employee”.
  • Show the approval moment. The human deciding is part of the story.
  • Use concrete SMB scenarios: the salon, the agency, the workshop, the clinic.
  • End marketing surfaces with Start free. Keep Book a call for teams that need more than Gold.

Don’t · messaging

  • Don’t call it a chatbot, copilot, or virtual assistant.
  • Don’t promise full autonomy or “set and forget”.
  • Don’t compare it to generic AI chat tools. Different category: an employee in your workflow.
  • Don’t use em dashes in copy. Short sentences instead.
  • Don’t hide the plans. Bronze, Silver and Gold and the free month are public; only custom pricing above Gold belongs to a call.
  • Don’t invent taglines or testimonials. Real proof only.

Don’t · logo & color

  • Don’t place the wordmark on low-contrast or busy backgrounds.
  • Don’t stretch, rotate, or restyle the wordmark or tile.
  • Don’t introduce colors outside the ten-swatch palette in official material.

09 · House rules

Things we never do

Learned the hard way while building the site. Each one exists because breaking it made the work worse.

Colour and form

  • This palette only. The swatches above are the whole system. No borrowed accent colours.
  • No emojis anywhere. Custom line icons instead, in mint on a pale tile.
  • One heading per section. No label pills above headings, no subheadings that restate the heading.
  • Uniform geometry. Matching widths and even gaps. Uneven sizing reads as unfinished.
  • Depth from shadow and light. Washes are welcome at the documented 8 to 32% strengths with ink text. Saturating whole cards is not.

Words

  • No em dashes. Short sentences instead.
  • No invented numbers. No customer counts, no hours-saved figures, no "most customers" claims until they are real.
  • No certification claims. Never write certified, compliant, audited, SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR or CCPA until a report exists.
  • No aphorisms. If a line sounds meaningful but names nothing, cut it. Name real jobs instead.
  • Plain over clever. A busy owner should follow every sentence in one pass.
The safety promises are load-bearing. The site tells visitors what Catilda can and cannot do. Every one of those statements has to be literally true of the build. If one stops being true, cut it from the site rather than soften the wording.
